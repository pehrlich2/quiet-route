Regulatory & licensing

Permits, rules and paperwork

Rules come from four levels: federal vehicle rules (because the truck and trailer together are rated over 10,001 lb and used for business), Vermont's waste laws, the county's solid waste district, and the towns. Staying under the commercial-license (CDL) line keeps the federal part light. None of it is hard, but all of it has to be done before the first paid pickup.

Federal vehicle rulesThese apply to "commercial motor vehicles": rated over 10,001 lb (truck, or truck plus trailer) and used in business

The Silverado alone may be rated right around 10,000 lb, and with either trailer we're well over. Vermont applies most federal truck-safety rules to trucks that never leave the state. Confirm exactly which ones with Vermont DMV's commercial vehicle enforcement office.

  • It's the ratings on the vehicles' stickers that count, not how much is loaded. Silverado (~10,000) + Trailer B (14,000) ≈ 24,000. Lightning (~8,300–8,600) + B ≈ 22,500. Both are under.
  • A trailer rated 10,000 lb or less never triggers Class A, no matter what tows it.
  • The traps: a heavier backup truck (anything rated over ~12,000 lb, such as a 3500 dually or an Isuzu NRR-EV) pulling Trailer B, or a trailer rated 16,000 lb. Also any garbage truck rated 26,001 lb or more. A Class 6 truck ordered at 25,995 lb avoids this.
  • What staying under saves: no drug and alcohol testing program, no CDL school (~$3–6k), and any licensed adult can be a backup driver.

Vermont waste law

Commercial hauler permit (Department of Environmental Conservation)

Anyone hauling solid waste for pay needs a permit, and every vehicle is registered on ANR Online. Permits last five years but must be renewed every year by May 1, for the permit year July 1 to June 30.

Universal Recycling Law (Act 148)

Haulers who collect household trash must:

  • Offer recycling collection of Vermont's required recyclables, kept separate from trash, and take it to a recycling facility (since 2015).
  • Not bill recycling as a separate line item on a residential customer's bill. It has to be built into the trash price.
  • Offer leaf and yard waste collection (since 2016) and food-scrap collection (since 2017), delivered to places that compost or otherwise reuse them. These can be priced as add-ons. Ask ANR how seasonal or add-on offerings satisfy "offer."
  • Price trash by volume or weight (see below).
  • Respect landfill bans: recyclables, leaf and yard waste, food scraps, electronics, paint, rechargeable batteries, mercury products and more. The transfer station can reject or fine loads that contain them.

A town that provides universal municipal collection, which residents can't opt out of, can take over these obligations. None of our four towns does, as far as we know. Sources: 10 V.S.A. § 6607a, DEC hauler FAQ.

Cart sizes and how we're allowed to bill

Vermont requires variable-rate pricing: residential trash has to be charged by volume or weight, so people who throw away more pay more. Flat "unlimited" pricing isn't allowed. Towns and districts pick the system and haulers follow it. CSWD requires each hauler to file its pricing system every year by May 1.

  • Recycling is included in every tier, with no separate fee. Compost and yard waste are priced separately.
  • Recycling carts are usually 64 or 96 gal. Size doesn't change the price, since recycling can't be billed separately.

Chittenden Solid Waste District (CSWD)

  • Hauler license required for anyone collecting or transporting trash, recycling or organics generated in Chittenden County. The ordinance was updated in February 2026, so read the current version.
  • Insurance minimums: liability of $100k per person and $300k per occurrence for injury, $100k for property damage, with CSWD named as an additional insured. Our commercial policy will be well above this.
  • Pricing filing by May 1 each year, and tonnage reporting as the license requires.
  • District fee on each ton of trash, collected through the dump fee. Plus the state's $6/ton tax.
  • Recycling: goes to CSWD's recycling facility in Williston. A new $38M facility is expected to open in January 2027. The hauler fee per ton was $25 in 2018. Ask for the current rate.

Checklist before the first paid pickup

  1. Form the company (LLC or co-op). Get an EIN and a Vermont tax account.
  2. Ask ANR about pricing tiers and what counts as "offering" compost and yard collection.
  3. Commercial auto, general liability and pollution insurance, with CSWD as additional insured.
  4. Get a USDOT number, mark the truck, get a DOT medical card, and start the driver file.
  5. Register the truck and trailer with Vermont DMV and get them inspected. Pay the 6% purchase and use tax.
  6. Vermont commercial hauler permit and vehicle registration on ANR Online.
  7. CSWD hauler license, pricing filing and reporting setup.
  8. Open hauler charge accounts at Casella Williston, CSWD recycling and Green Mountain Compost. Get each current rate sheet in writing, and ask how much notice they give before price changes (see below).
  9. Zoning check for the yard.
  10. Attorney review of the customer terms.

This outline is our own reading of public sources, not legal advice. Confirm each item with the agency named.
