How service works

This outline will become the customer-facing terms. Every rule here exists either because Vermont requires it, to keep a driver safe, or because it prevents the most common hauler complaints. Keep the final version short enough that people actually read it.

Vermont's Universal Recycling Law requires haulers who collect household trash to offer recycling, leaf and yard, and food-scrap collection. Recycling can't be billed as a separate line item. Compost and yard waste can be charged as add-ons. Details on the Regulatory page.

What we can actually see: the tipper empties a closed cart in seconds, so we can't inspect every bag. We act on what's visible: items set beside the cart, anything on top when the lid opens, and things the box camera sees land in the trailer (propane tanks, paint cans, bagged recyclables). When we leave something, or see a banned item go in, you get a tag and a photo explaining why and where it should go instead. For everything else, the rules rely on customers. If a hidden banned item damages equipment or gets our load rejected (a lithium battery fire, for example), we may charge the cost. CSWD's A–Z guide is the reference to link customers to.

Where to put carts

  • At the end of the driveway, by 7am on your day. Put it out the night before only if you're not in a bear area. Otherwise, morning of.
  • Lid opening facing the street, handle toward the house. That's how the tipper grabs it.
  • 3 ft of clearance from mailboxes, cars, poles, and the other cart. The lifter needs room to swing.
  • On a flat, plowed surface, not on top of or behind a snowbank. In winter, clear a spot at the end of the plowed driveway.
  • Lid closed, nothing on top, under ~150 lb. Overfilled or overweight carts can't be tipped safely.
  • Bring it back in the same day. This keeps carts from getting plowed, blown over or knocked over by bears.
  • Private roads and long driveways: we'll look at the site before the first pickup and agree on a pickup spot.

Schedule and missed pickups

  • Holidays and weather: the route slides one day, with a Saturday make-up day. You'll get an alert the evening before and the morning of.
  • If we missed you: tell us by noon the next business day. We'll come back within one business day at no charge.
  • If the cart wasn't out: the GPS shows the truck at your address and the tipper camera takes a timestamped photo of the curb, so both of us can see what happened. You can book a return trip (~$15) or wait for your next day.
  • Vacation holds: pause for 2+ weeks with no charge for those pickups, up to 3 months a year.

Our guaranteeSimple, specific, and cheap to honor

Good guarantees from small service businesses share a few features: a specific promise, an automatic remedy, and no arguing. Our draft:

Missed pickup, next one free

If we miss you and don't make it right within a business day, your next pickup is free, applied automatically.

No contracts

Month to month. Cancel online anytime with a prorated refund of any prepaid amount. Founding members keep their locked price as long as they stay.

We always tell you why

If we don't take something, you get a tag and a photo in the app, never a mystery.

We fix what we break

If we damage a mailbox, a cart or your lawn, we repair or replace it. No claim forms under $500.

30 days' notice on price changes

No surprise fees. We don't add fuel surcharges, because the truck is electric.

Quiet hours

No pickups before 7am. The electric truck and tipper are quiet anyway.

Billing

  • Autopay in advance, monthly or quarterly, by card or bank account. Prices are by cart size (32, 64 or 96 gal), as Vermont requires.
  • Failed payment: reminders at 3 and 10 days. Service pauses after 30 days, and we pick up the cart after 60.
  • Extras: overflow bag tags, bulky items, return trips, and cart size changes (one free change a year).
  • Carts belong to Quiet Route and have serial numbers. Replacement is free for normal wear, theft (with a police report) or bear damage. You pay for damage you cause.
  • Moving: give 2 weeks' notice. We transfer service to the new address if we serve it. The cart stays with the house.
  • Prepaid founding members: held in a separate account until your zone launches. Automatic full refund if it doesn't launch by the posted date.

Contamination

  • Recycling: a tag and photo showing what was wrong. After three tagged pickups, the cart is left until it's sorted. Bagged recyclables and plastic film are the most common problems.
  • Compost: buckets with plastic, glass or metal get a tag instead of a pickup. Green Mountain Compost fines haulers $75–150 a ton for contaminated loads, so this matters.
  • Trash with banned items: left with a tag saying where the item goes instead.

Access, safety and conduct

  • Assisted pickup: free for customers who can't move a cart because of age or disability. The driver walks the cart from the house and returns it.
  • Dogs: please keep them inside on your pickup day. If a dog makes it unsafe, the driver skips the stop and tells you.
  • Drivers stay near the curb except for assisted pickup. They don't open garages or gates unless you've arranged it.
  • Complaints and disputes: reply to any message or use the website. A person answers within one business day. The owner reviews any refund request that's refused.

Privacy

  • The live map shows only the truck, and only while it's on a route. It never shows other customers' addresses.
  • Photos are of the cart and the curb, used only to explain a skipped or tagged pickup. Deleted after 90 days.
  • We never sell customer data. Text messages only if you opt in, and "STOP" always works.

What else to decide

  • Service area rules: who pays extra for long private roads or seasonal camps.
  • Price for move-in and move-out cleanouts, a good seasonal business with student rentals.
  • Commercial customers: a separate one-page agreement with a 1-year term and 30 days' notice to cancel.
  • Legal review: have a Vermont attorney turn this outline into terms of service. Pay attention to auto-renewal and refund wording under Vermont consumer protection law.
