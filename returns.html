Return = (profit − replacement set-aside, then − tax for the after-tax figure) ÷ cash invested, per year. Payback = cash invested ÷ that monthly amount. After-tax figures are illustrative, using 2025 federal and 2024 Vermont rules for one example owner. They aren't tax advice.

Notes on taxes

  • Pass-through: as an LLC (or S-corp), profit is taxed on the owners' personal returns at their own rates. That's why the after-tax figure depends on the owner's other income.
  • Self-employment tax: an LLC owner pays it on profit. It's mostly Medicare once other wages pass the Social Security cap. An S-corp avoids it on profit beyond a reasonable salary, but adds payroll and admin costs. It usually pays off only at higher profits, or when the owner has no other wages.
  • 20% small-business deduction (Section 199A) applies to the profit, subject to wage limits at higher incomes. A hired driver's wages help keep it.
  • Year 1: the equipment can be deducted all at once federally. Vermont doesn't follow that and uses regular 5-year depreciation.
  • Active vs passive: a passive investor may also owe the 3.8% net investment income tax at higher incomes. Active owners don't on business income.
